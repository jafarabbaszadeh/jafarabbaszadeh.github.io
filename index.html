About me

Welcome to my homepage! I recently defended my Ph.D. dissertation in Aerospace Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign, where I am affiliated with the Coordinated Science Laboratory. I work on sequential decision-making under uncertainty — at the intersection of online learning, dynamic optimization, control theory, and game theory — with an emphasis on finite-time, high-probability guarantees that a decision-maker can rely on before the data arrives.

My doctoral research asks what guarantees a learning decision-maker should provide beyond regret. I design algorithms whose guarantees hold at every point in time rather than only at a horizon fixed in advance, quantify the trade-off between regret and safety, and develop commitment rules for environments that change regime. I also study decision-making under safety sets and constraints that are themselves learned from data, and what performance guarantees survive when the learning data is corrupted by an adversary. My thesis is Safety-Aware Learning-Based Control.

My current interests extend toward decision-making with mixed discrete and continuous actions — which option to commit to, and how intensely to act on it. On the application side, I am drawn to high-stakes operational settings such as energy systems, robotics, and decision-making in healthcare.

I am currently on the job market for postdoctoral positions. If you have an opening in online learning, control, or sequential decision-making, I would be glad to hear from you at jafar2@illinois.edu.

Research Interests

  • Mixed discrete–continuous decision-making: sequential problems in which one must simultaneously choose among discrete alternatives and optimize continuous actions, including extensions to interacting decision-makers.
  • Any-time learning and decision-making: algorithms with finite-time, high-probability guarantees that hold throughout the learning process, without requiring a prespecified horizon.
  • Performance–safety trade-offs: understanding how much performance must be sacrificed to obtain stronger control of worst-case system behavior, and characterizing this trade-off explicitly.
  • Learning and decision-making across regime changes: problems in which the decision-maker must choose when to switch, how long to commit to a regime, and how learning guarantees depend on the number and cost of consequential transitions.
  • Learning under uncertain safety sets/constraints: sequential decisions in which feasibility or safety constraints are themselves unknown and must be learned from data.
  • Robust learning under adversarial and corrupted data: defending learning systems against adversarial attacks on datasets and data corruption, with performance guarantees that explicitly depend on the adversary's corruption budget.
